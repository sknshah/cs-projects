Background

Battleship is a classic guessing game for two players. It started out as a pencil-and-paper game that people played by hand, long before it became a mass-produced board game. Milton Bradley released the plastic grid version most people picture in 1967, and the same basic idea has since shown up in video games, apps, and even a feature film. The rules below describe the simplified, single-player version implemented on this page.

The Idea

Each side secretly places a fleet of ships on a grid. On your turn you call out a coordinate on your opponent's grid, trying to guess where their ships are hiding. Your opponent tells you whether that shot was a hit or a miss. Once every square a ship occupies has been hit, that ship is sunk. The first player to sink the other side's entire fleet wins.

The Rules Used Here

  • The board is an 8-by-8 grid, columns A through H and rows 1 through 8.
  • Your fleet and the computer's fleet are placed randomly and secretly at the start of each game.
  • The fleet has 4 ships: a Carrier (4 squares), a Cruiser (3 squares), a Submarine (3 squares), and a Destroyer (2 squares).
  • Ships are placed in a straight line, horizontally or vertically, and never overlap another ship.
  • You and the computer take turns. Click a square on the "Enemy Waters" board to fire at that square.
  • A red square means a hit, a light square means a miss. Once every square of a ship has been hit, it is announced as sunk.
  • The game ends the moment one side's entire fleet has been sunk.
